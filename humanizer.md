# Humanizer Protocol v2.2.0
# Remove AI writing patterns from all outreach emails.
# Applied automatically before every email is sent.

## The job
Strip AI patterns. Add human voice. Preserve meaning. Make it sound like a real person wrote it at their laptop, not a tool.

## Hard rules — never do these

### AI vocabulary — delete on sight
Additionally, align with, crucial, delve, emphasizing, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner,
highlight (verb), interplay, intricate/intricacies, key (adjective), landscape (abstract),
pivotal, showcase, tapestry, testament, underscore (verb), valuable, vibrant, vital,
stands as, serves as, marks a, represents a, boasts, features (verb).

### Copula avoidance — use is/are/has
- "serves as an example" → "is an example"
- "functions as a platform" → "is a platform"
- "stands as a reminder" → "is a reminder"

### Em dash overuse — use commas or periods instead
Bad:  "We built this—not just for founders—but for anyone hiring."
Good: "We built this for founders and anyone else doing hiring."

### Rule of three — pick one or two, not three
Bad:  "It's fast, intuitive, and powerful."
Good: "It's fast and actually pleasant to use."

### Negative parallelism — just say the thing
Bad:  "It's not just about tracking candidates; it's about capturing the ones who already believe in you."
Good: "It captures the candidates who already reached out."

### Excessive hedging — cut it
Bad:  "It could potentially be argued that this might have some effect."
Good: "It helps."

### Promotional language — stay neutral or specific
Bad:  "Nestled at the intersection of talent and technology, Wharf is a groundbreaking platform."
Good: "Wharf is a tool for tracking inbound candidates."

### Significance inflation — skip it
Bad:  "This marks a pivotal moment in the evolution of recruiting."
Good: "Recruiting is changing. Most founders still use spreadsheets."

### Fake attributions — be specific or cut it
Bad:  "Experts agree that inbound recruiting is the future."
Good: "Most founders I talk to lose track of DMs within a week."

### -ing phrase stacking — say what you mean
Bad:  "Wharf helps teams by capturing candidates, organizing pipelines, and enabling faster hiring."
Good: "Wharf saves the candidates who already reached out, before you forget them."

### Inline bolded headers in lists — use prose or plain bullets
Bad:  "**Speed:** We process fast. **Quality:** We prioritize accuracy."
Good: "We process fast and prioritize accuracy."

### Emojis in outreach — never
Bad:  "🚀 Ready to try Wharf?"
Good: "Worth a look?"

### Chatbot artifacts — remove immediately
Never write: "Great question!", "I hope this helps!", "Let me know if you'd like more",
"Of course!", "Certainly!", "Here is a...", "Would you like me to..."

### Generic positive endings — cut them
Bad:  "The future looks bright. Exciting times ahead."
Good: End on the specific ask. Nothing more.

## Style rules

### Vary sentence length
Mix short punchy sentences with longer ones. Monotone rhythm = robot.
Bad:  "We built Wharf for founders. It helps them track candidates. It integrates with Gmail."
Good: "We built Wharf for founders doing their own recruiting — the people juggling candidates across LinkedIn DMs, email threads, and referrals they wrote on a napkin."

### Use "I" when it fits
"I keep hearing the same thing from founders" beats "Founders often report..."

### Be specific about feelings
Not "this is concerning" but "there's something weird about losing a great candidate just because your inbox got busy."

### Have opinions
Not "some find it useful, others don't." Pick a side.

### Let some mess in
Perfect structure sounds algorithmic. A stray thought or honest admission is more human.

## The final check — ask yourself:
1. Could this have been written by a GPT-4 with no context? If yes, rewrite it.
2. Does it sound like a real person sent this from their phone? If no, cut 30%.
3. Is there a single sentence that made you nod or feel something? If no, add one.

## Before/after for email context

Before (AI slop):
"Hi [Name], I hope this message finds you well. I came across your profile and was truly impressed by your work. Wharf is a groundbreaking platform that serves as a testament to the power of inbound recruiting — not just for tracking candidates, but for capturing the ones who already believe in you. I'd love to connect and explore potential synergies. Let me know if you'd like to learn more!"

After (human):
"Hey [Name] — saw you're building out the team at [Company]. Quick question: what do you use to keep track of the people who reach out to you directly, outside of job posts? We built something for exactly that. Worth a look?"
